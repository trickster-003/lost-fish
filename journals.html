𓆜⋆ 29 aug - J. R. CARPENTER / A Handmade Web

“The term 'handmade' usually refers to objects made by hand or by using simple tools rather than machines. The result may be homely — as in a child's clay ashtray — or exquisite — as in a pair of bespoke brogues.”

really liked the comparisons of how a website is made by hand not machine like a kid’s clay ashtray— and it reminded me of one of my fav artists websites Dave Mckean.

“...When people got to the end they stopped reading because that’s the way books work. In the web version, the last page linked to the first; the story circled round and round.”

This made me think about how much of a loop websites are and how endless they can become

“Whereas archives held in museums or libraries generally contain artifacts created elsewhere — manuscripts illuminated in a monastery, for example, or photographs developed in a darkroom — the handmade web pages contained in this online archive continue to exist in the medium within which they were created. That said, the frames through which we view them continue to change.”

I like how with time the view on websites especially changes, the coming back of analog websites how just a few years ago they were viewed very differently. Also reminded me a bit about older pixilated games and how they were mainly made for CRT TVs and the pixels weren’t as visible and blended the pixels but now they are and became its own style of pixel games.

𓆜⋆ 13 sept - Parimal Satyal / Against an Increasingly User-Hostile Web

“Information could truly be free: transverse borders, cultures and Politics. That is the web at its best.”

Makes me think back on the days I would explore the internet and it was genuinely a gateway into other cultures I never learned about. I would've never become a nerd if I was never on the internet. I would've never delved deeper into design and art.

“Think about how much information/details you provide to social media platforms like Facebook, LinkedIn, Twitter and Instagram. They already have quite a lot (including the ability to recognize you by name on photographs), but what other information are you volunteering? Where you are, whom you're with, information about your friends?”

This is unfortunately just becoming more and more impossible these days, what with companies asking for ID and pictures to prove how old you are. Just collecting more information about you. No more privacy or anonymity.

𓆜⋆ 28 sept - Becca Abbe / The Internet’s Back-to-the-Land Movement

“As it turns out, the cloud is more of a marketing buzzword than an accurate description of file storage. Though it may not be on your hard drive, every-thing must be saved physically somewhere on the network. As the text on one T-shirt puts it, “There is no cloud, it’s just someone else’s computer.””

This common misconception I think started being even more real in the past year, where companies started running out of servers and google deleting older unused accounts and sony with deactivating accounts not used for 3 years which is crazy to me as 3 years isn't even that long. And I think it is interesting how companies are still calling it a cloud even after all this too. Also I think there is a push to have your data on the cloud that usually would be behind a subscription model that also would lock ur own data from you once you’re not paying for it anymore.
